# Three Firings

*An algorithmic philosophy, made with the algorithmic-art skill.*

A surface is not drawn; it is fired. This movement treats an image as a record of an atmosphere. The painter's marks are only an intention: a mask of where slip was laid thick or thin. What the viewer finally sees is decided by a simulated kiln. In it, an invisible field of oxygen breathes across the work three times, turning some regions black and glossy, letting others return to the warm red of clay, and leaving smoky grey clouds where the fire was starved. Beauty lives in this negotiation between a precise intention and an indifferent atmosphere. It is the product of deep computational patience: a meticulously crafted algorithm in which every constant has been tuned until the accidents look inevitable.

The intention is drawn as geometry: concentric bands, running keys, tongues, lattices and rising streams. It is stored as a layered mask of four materials: bare clay, glaze, added red and added white. Antialiased edges are not errors but blends, read as partial thicknesses of paint. Every stroke wavers by a few pixels under a slow noise field, the way a brush loaded on a turning wheel never quite holds its line. Particles are the brush's other hand. Seeded streams rise through a curl-like flow field and are laid down as fine reserved lines. Nets of cords sag over domes with knots that drift from their ideal lattice. Rows of slots are filled with tokens by a seeded lot. The geometry is exact, its execution human, its variation entirely reproducible from a single seed: the mark of a master-level implementation.

The firing is a field process. A low-frequency oxygen map, built from several octaves of noise sampled at a fraction of the resolution and smoothly interpolated, decides how completely each region reduces and reoxidises. Where glaze is thick and the atmosphere is right, it sinters to a deep, faintly blue-black lustre. Where it is thin and oxygen returns, it misfires to brown-red, an honest flaw worth keeping. Clay modulates through its own iron-rich mottling and fine grit, and darkens into soft grey fire-clouds where the oxygen map dips. A gloss term sweeps a soft highlight across glazed regions only, so black reads as material rather than void. Every one of these responses is the result of painstaking optimisation, balanced so that no single effect announces itself.

Time is held still, then released. The system renders once per seed, as a kiln opens once per load, yet within that single pass a fine crazing network grows: short seeded random walks that branch and stop. They are drawn faintly enough to be found only on a second look. Density, misfire, brush tremor and the strength of the fire-clouds are the tunable parameters. Each is a lever on the atmosphere, not on the picture, so that any setting still yields a coherent fired object. This restraint is part of the craftsmanship: the controls were chosen through countless iterations so that the space of seeds is wide, and every point in it looks considered.

Composition comes from the vessel, not the frame. Circular tondos, banded friezes and rectangular panels are the only containers. Inside them, rhythm comes from repetition: the key, the tongue, the slot, the knot. Surprise comes from the field. The result should feel like something lifted from a museum vitrine and then quietly animated by mathematics, the work of a meticulously crafted algorithm from someone at the very top of computational craft, where every seed is a different firing of the same pot.
